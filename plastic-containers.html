SECTION 2 - QUICK ALTERNATIVE

When You're On The Go

No sink nearby? You can still make a responsible disposal decision in a few quick steps.

1

Empty the container

Put leftover food and liquids into the trash. Remove utensils and other loose objects.

2

Wipe away residue

Use a napkin to scrape or wipe away sauce and food. A spotless container isn't required, but it must be empty and reasonably clean.

3

Make the bin decision

If food or liquid still remains and you can't clean it, use the trash. If it is empty and clean enough, check the plastic type and local recycling rules before using a recycling bin.

Important: Not every recycling bin accepts every plastic container. Check the bin label and your building or city's rules. Do not place food-filled containers in recycling.

Source: Recycle by City - Cleaning Recyclables

SECTION 4 - REDUCE

Use Fewer Single-Use Containers

The best container to recycle is the one you never had to throw out.

1

Bring your own container

Use reusable dishes and containers for packed lunches and leftovers. If a restaurant allows it, ask them to fill your own container for takeout.

2

Skip the extras you won't use

Say no to plastic utensils at takeout when you don't need them. If you do keep them, wash and reuse them.

3

Choose less packaging

Pick items with less packaging, and buy in bulk so you can refill your own container.

Reduce comes first: every container you avoid is one you don't have to clean, sort, or find a bin for.

Source: EPA - What You Can Do To Reduce Plastic Waste

SECTION 5 - REUSE

Give Your Container A Second Life

A sturdy container that is still in good shape can be used again before it is recycled.

1

Keep it for lunch or leftovers

Wash it well and use it again, as long as it is not cracked or warped. Thin takeout containers are usually made for one use, so do not microwave or heat them unless the label says it is microwave-safe.

2

Use it to store small things

Clean, dry containers can hold desk supplies, cables, craft items or dry snacks.

3

Pass it on

If it is still in good shape, give it to someone who can use it, or donate it.

Retire it when: it cracks, warps, or stays stained or smelly. Then follow the steps in Section 1 and recycle it.

Source: EPA - What You Can Do To Reduce Plastic Waste
